## The premise

**Chicago, 1972.** A reserved, exacting Black man takes over a **general store** in an invented South Side neighborhood — flour, lightbulbs, fishing line, everything and nothing — and begins curating it, object by object, into something finer. A tin-lined copper pan appears next to the motor oil. A carbon-steel knife where the church candles were.

The neighborhood watches their ordinary store become something they didn't ask for. Some love it. Some feel judged by it. **The store's transformation is the series arc, and every new object on a shelf is a small referendum.**

His thesis: elevate the kitchen, elevate the mind. The show loves him for it and never endorses it.

## The world posture

Real Chicago — the lake, the winters, the El, the South Side as a real place. **Everything inside it is invented:** the neighborhood, every institution, every person. See `bible/world/fictionalization.md` (the skeleton rule). Research skeleton: `research/south-side-1970s-dossier.md` — dynamics are true, flesh is ours.

## The protagonist

Invented whole; unnamed for now. Reserved, exacting, private in a way that invites theories — the documented *absence* in the real seed becomes his texture, not his biography. Warmth shown through exactitude. He teaches people to want things they have no words for, and teaching them about an object, teaches something harder to name.

## The triangle

Three ways the dream reaches the neighborhood:

1. **The Magazine** — a glossy Black monthly with a gleaming test kitchen downtown. It *prints* the dream.
2. **The store (ours)** — *teaches* the dream, one object and one name at a time. Inefficient on purpose; the inefficiency is the product.
3. **The Store downtown** — a grand department store that *mass-produces* the dream, stripped of the teaching, at volume and lower price.

The season pressure runs on 2 vs. 3: taste meets scale, and the story watches what scale costs taste. The antagonist is the downtown store's face — charming, genuinely great at retail, and possibly right (see `bible/inbox.md`, ready for promotion).

## The guard (canon, not advice)

The elevation thesis sits on a real fault line — liberation and discipline in the same gesture. Therefore, as a rule of the show:

- The strongest critique of the protagonist's thesis belongs to **sympathetic characters who sometimes win the scene** — first among them a soul-food cook whose expertise the show treats as equal knowledge, never as raw material for his refinement.
- Two cuisines' worth of knowledge meet. Neither elevates the other.
- If every character ends up agreeing he's right, the show has curdled into a sermon. Rebalance.

## Era

Starts ~1972. The neighborhood's last golden moment — and he can feel it.
