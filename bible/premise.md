**Status: unchosen.** Do not write beats, characters, or world detail that assumes a setting until this file is filled. Everything below is the frame the premise must satisfy, not the premise itself.

## The engine (fixed, premise-agnostic)

A reserved, exacting person teaches ordinary people to want well-made things they have no words for — and, teaching them about an object, teaches them about something harder to name. Taste meets scale; the story watches what scale costs taste. This survives any change of protagonist, city, era, or trade.

## The tonal target (fixed)

Warm, not sentimental. Comedy from objects and misunderstanding, not quips. See `CLAUDE.md` §3.

## What the premise must decide

- **Protagonist.** Who is the exacting teacher? Name, age, background. (Free to differ from the real Chuck Williams in every respect.)
- **Place & era.** Where and when? (Live example, not a commitment: a Black proprietor on the South Side of Chicago in the 1970s.)
- **The trade.** What does the store sell? Cookware is the touchstone; it need not be the choice. Whatever it is must reward the "single object as a lesson" move.
- **The ensemble.** The town / block / room the store gathers.
- **The pressure.** What force represents "scale" — a chain, a developer, a franchise offer, a changing neighborhood?

## How this gets filled

Develop 2–3 distinct reimaginings as short premises, choose one, then write it here. Only then does `bible/world/`, `bible/characters/`, and Structure Lab work begin. See `CLAUDE.md` §1, §4.
