## Steve Schubert — the department store
**Type:** `Character` (antagonist) · **Edge:** `pressures →` the store · **Captured:** 2026-08-06

**Function — the personification of *scale*.** Owns the big department store downtown. He is the force the show is actually about, given a face: the recurring antagonist who subverts, appropriates, and outright steals the protagonist's ideas.

**The engine of the antagonism (the strong part):** he treats the small shop as **an R&D lab he doesn't have to pay for.** He watches what the protagonist teaches people to want, waits for it to sell, then runs it at volume and undercuts on price. Historically true of department stores, and thematically exact — scale eating taste. Every theft is also, humiliatingly, a compliment.

**Physicality (as captured):** skinny, big-nosed, effeminate, Poindexter-ish — a bureaucrat, not a brute; unthreatening in the body, dangerous in the ledger.

> ⚠️ **Flag before this goes to script.** That specific cluster — big nose + effeminate + steals + department-store owner + a Shubert-adjacent surname — lands as antisemitic caricature and sissy-coded villainy, whatever the intent. It's also the weaker choice dramatically: coding a villain as physically off-putting does the work that menace should do, and lets the audience dismiss him. See "the sharper version" below. Keep the *bureaucrat, not brute* instinct — that part is good.

**The sharper version (proposed, not adopted):** make him **charming, well-dressed, genuinely good at retail — and right about scale.** He isn't stealing because he's a weasel; he's stealing because he recognizes genius faster than anyone else in the city, and he can *actually deliver it to a hundred thousand people* while the protagonist serves forty. The unbearable part isn't that he's repellent — it's that he has a case. An antagonist you can't dismiss is the one that makes the theme hurt.

His threat then reads as: *"You're teaching one town to want this. I can teach a country. Why is that the villain's line?"*

**Open questions**
- Does he *want* the protagonist's respect? (Much richer if yes — the thief who wants to be admired by the man he robs.)
- Rival, or eventual buyer/partner? The "scale" pressure is strongest if he's a standing offer, not just a raid.
- Name is placeholder; check it against real department-store figures before locking (`legal/notes.md`).
