# The skeleton rule — what's real, what's ours

The research (`research/south-side-1970s-dossier.md`) is a skeleton: we keep the **dynamics**, we invent the **flesh**. Nothing on screen is a documented thing; everything on screen is true to documented things.

## Real (borrowed as ground)

- **The city.** Chicago itself — geography, weather, the El, the lake, the fact of the South Side.
- **The era.** ~1972: a Black middle-class neighborhood's last golden moment; downtown retail beginning its long decline; collective-uplift movements rising; credit discrimination as a standing fact.
- **The dynamics.** The elevation-vs-respectability fault line; the dream printed / taught / mass-produced triangle; import arbitrage and curation-as-unpaid-R&D; the small shop as the room the whole block passes through.

## Rules

1. **Real institutions never act in the story.** They may exist as unremarked background texture (a magazine on a table, a shopping bag), nothing more. Our invented analogues carry all plot.
2. **Analogues are composites, not costumes.** The Store downtown draws on documented dynamics from several real stores; it is not any one of them renamed. Same for every analogue.
3. **Invented names must not collide** with the real analogue or living people — check before locking (`legal/notes.md`).
4. **When research and drama disagree, drama wins — on purpose and on record.** Note deliberate departures here or in the relevant bible page. The knockoff engine itself is already flagged in the research as plausible invention: fine — it's ours.
