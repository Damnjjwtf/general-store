# TV & Streaming corpus — orientation

An **additive** extension to Structure Lab's library, for series work. The Lab's existing library is film-shaped: its 30 systems are arrangement logics (Three-Act, Kishōtenketsu, Rashomon, ritual-calendar…) that assume a single closed text. A series is a different animal — it must *keep producing itself*.

> **Additive only.** Nothing here modifies the co-owned 30-system structure corpus (ids 1–30) or the 20 tone cards (ids 101–120). New id ranges are chosen so they cannot collide.

## Three decks, because TV conflates three axes

Most TV structure advice mixes these up, which is why "what's your structure?" gets answered with a runtime. They're independent — you pick one from each.

| Deck | Ids | Answers | File |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Format** | 201–212 | What is the container, and how is it delivered? | `format-cards.md` |
| **Structure** | 301–315 | How is story *arranged* inside it? | `structure-cards.md` |
| **Engine** | 401–414 | What renewably *generates* episodes? | `engine-cards.md` |

**Engine is the important one.** Film has no equivalent — a movie needs a story, a series needs a machine that makes stories. It is the single most common thing missing from a pilot that reads well and dies at episode 8. A series without an engine is a pilot with a sequel problem.

## Card schema

Matches the Lab's existing structure cards exactly, so these paste straight into the library:

- `mechanism` — how it works, stated so a stranger could apply it
- `engineers` — **the audience state it manufactures.** Not what it does; what it *does to the viewer*
- `example` — a real show that runs on it
- `failsWhen` — the specific way it dies in execution

## How the Lab should use these

Under the existing synthesis method, these decks slot into the morphological box as parameters — **`format-logic`, `arrangement-logic`, `engine-logic`** — joining time/POV/space/knowledge/ritual/unit-of-repetition/tone. The existing rules still govern:

- **Bisociation** — a candidate must cross ≥2 families. A Format + Structure + Engine triple already crosses three decks; the bar is that the *combination* be non-obvious (Institution engine + Bottle structure is a cliché; Threshold engine + Dual-Era braid is not).
- **Tone stays a full parameter.** A series' tonal engine must key to a structural feature — for TV that usually means the act-out, the cold open, or the POV handoff.
- **Kill rules apply**, plus two series-specific ones:
  - **No engine → kill.** If you can't say what generates episode 12, it isn't a series.
  - **Detachable format → kill.** If the format could be swapped (4-act → premium hour) without changing the beat-map, format isn't load-bearing, it's a runtime.

## Weighting for one-hour character dramedy

Full space is covered, but these are the hot cards for the lane *General Store* sits in — warm, ensemble, object-driven, workplace-as-home:

**Format** 203 (premium hour), 205 (half-hour single-cam), 207 (cold open as thesis), 210 (weekly release)
**Structure** 301 (A/B/C braid), 302 (kernel & satellite), 303 (self-completing + spine), 307 (POV rotation)
**Engine** 402 (Threshold), 413 (Craft), 409 (Territory), 414 (Rivalry), 406 (Chosen Family)

Note 402 × 413 is close to the show's stated engine already — *a shop the world walks into, run by someone whose mastery of a trade is the weekly problem.* Which is a signal the Lab should be made to **fight**: the obvious combination is the one bisociation exists to interrogate.

## Deliberate omissions

Where a TV card would duplicate a base-corpus system, it is **not** re-added — the base card is used with a series note instead. Rashomon (multi-POV retelling), ritual-calendar, and the act paradigms already live at ids 1–30. `structure-cards.md` flags these as *use base card* rather than cloning them.

## Sources & currency

Built from established series craft — Neil Landau's showrunner work (*The TV Showrunner's Roadmap*, *TV Outside the Box*), John Yorke on fractal act structure, narratology's kernel/satellite distinction, and Mittell on complex serialization — plus verification of the current streaming-format picture (act breaks eliminated then partly returning with ad-supported tiers; 8–13 episode seasons; release cadence as a structural choice).

**Currency caveat:** buyer format preferences move fast. Anything here about *market* preference should be re-verified before it drives a pitch decision; the structural mechanics are stable.
