# Structure deck — ids 301–315

How story is *arranged* inside the container. TV-native arrangement systems.

> **No duplicates.** Where a TV move is really an existing base-corpus system applied to series, it is **not** re-added — see "Use the base card" at the end.

---

### 301 · A/B/C Braid
- **mechanism:** Two to four storylines run in parallel in *different emotional registers*, cut against each other and rhymed at act-outs. Classically: A carries the engine, B a relationship, C a comic runner.
- **engineers:** Relief and resonance. The viewer is moved between registers so no single tone fatigues, and meaning arrives through juxtaposition rather than statement.
- **example:** *The Good Place*
- **failsWhen:** The strands never rhyme — three stories share a runtime but not a subject, and the episode is a bundle rather than a whole.

### 302 · Kernel & Satellite Economy
- **mechanism:** Every scene is classed: **kernels** advance and cannot be cut; **satellites** affirm relationship, clarify, or introduce. Satellites keep a strand alive at low cost.
- **engineers:** The sense that a world is continuously present, even when it isn't advancing. Characters persist between their plot beats.
- **example:** *Friday Night Lights*
- **failsWhen:** Satellite drift — a season of affirmation with no kernels. Everyone is lovingly observed and nothing occurs.

### 303 · Self-Completing Unit over a Spine
- **mechanism:** Each episode poses and resolves its own problem (case, customer, job) while a serialized spine advances underneath, usually at act-out and tag.
- **engineers:** Satisfaction *and* investment — the weekly closure that makes an episode re-watchable, plus the debt that brings the viewer back.
- **example:** *Bones*, *Ted Lasso*
- **failsWhen:** The two decouple. The weekly unit calcifies into formula and the spine starves — or the spine eats the unit and the show forgets how to end an hour.

### 304 · Bottle Episode
- **mechanism:** Confine the cast to one location for one episode. Remove plot machinery; force character to supply the pressure.
- **engineers:** Exposure. With nowhere to go, the viewer watches people rather than events — and sees what's actually between them.
- **example:** *Mad Men*, "The Suitcase"
- **failsWhen:** Deployed for budget, not need. Confinement without an unresolved relationship is just a cheaper episode.

### 305 · Flashback Counterpoint
- **mechanism:** A present-tense A-line braided with a past-tense line that comments on it — recurring architecture, not a one-off device.
- **engineers:** Dramatic irony as a standing condition. The viewer knows more than the characters and reads every present scene through a past one.
- **example:** *Lost*
- **failsWhen:** The past line becomes exposition delivery — backstory dispensed on schedule rather than an argument with the present.

### 306 · Dual-Era Season Braid
- **mechanism:** Two time periods run as co-equal seasons-within-a-season, converging or refusing to.
- **engineers:** Suspense about *meaning* rather than event — the viewer works to see how the eras rhyme.
- **example:** *The Crown* (era succession), *Yellowjackets* (interleaved)
- **failsWhen:** One era is obviously the real show. The other becomes an obligation the audience waits out.

### 307 · POV Rotation
- **mechanism:** Each episode centers a different ensemble member; the world stays fixed, the lens moves.
- **engineers:** Ensemble equity and re-reading — a character read as minor becomes the protagonist, retroactively recoloring earlier episodes.
- **example:** *Orange Is the New Black*
- **failsWhen:** Rotation becomes obligation. A character gets an hour because it's their turn, not because their view changes anything.

### 308 · Anthology-Within-Series
- **mechanism:** Self-contained episodes sharing a world, premise, or thematic question rather than a cast or continuity.
- **engineers:** Essayistic attention — the viewer reads for the *argument* across episodes rather than for what happens next.
- **example:** *Black Mirror*
- **failsWhen:** The connective question is too loose to feel like one show, and quality variance reads as inconsistency rather than range.

### 309 · Real-Time Episode
- **mechanism:** Story time equals screen time for an episode (or a series).
- **engineers:** Physical urgency. The viewer's own clock is conscripted; no relief through ellipsis.
- **example:** *24*
- **failsWhen:** The premise demands events that can't plausibly fit the clock, and the show starts cheating the very constraint that sells it.

### 310 · Season-as-Novel
- **mechanism:** Chapters, not episodes. Individual hours don't self-complete; the season is the smallest whole unit.
- **engineers:** Long-form absorption and the trust that setup will pay — the viewer forgives an inconclusive hour.
- **example:** *The Wire*
- **failsWhen:** Paired with weekly release or a casual audience. Without the binge or a patient viewer, "chapter four" reads as "nothing happened."

### 311 · Mosaic Ensemble
- **mechanism:** Many storylines with no privileged protagonist, intersecting institutionally rather than personally. The *system* is the subject.
- **engineers:** Sociological vision — the viewer sees structure and causation the characters can't.
- **example:** *The Wire*
- **failsWhen:** Breadth without an anchor. No one to care about, so the argument is admired and not felt.

### 312 · Cliffhanger Chain
- **mechanism:** Each episode ends mid-action or on a reversal, with no resting closure.
- **engineers:** Compulsion — the autoplay countdown wins every time.
- **example:** *Money Heist*
- **failsWhen:** Reversals must escalate to keep working, so the show inflates until nothing lands; or resolutions cheat the hooks that sold them.

### 313 · Framing Device Season
- **mechanism:** The season is told from inside a retrospective structure — a trial, a deposition, an interview, an inquest.
- **engineers:** Interpretive tension. The viewer knows an outcome exists and reads everything as evidence.
- **example:** *American Crime Story: The People v. O. J. Simpson*
- **failsWhen:** The frame is decorative. If it never constrains what can be shown or believed, it's a wrapper, not a structure.

### 314 · Inter-Season Time Jump
- **mechanism:** Substantial time passes between seasons; the show returns to changed circumstances and lets the gap do the work.
- **engineers:** Curiosity as a season-opening engine — the viewer reconstructs what happened, which is more active than being told.
- **example:** *Battlestar Galactica* ("New Caprica")
- **failsWhen:** The jump discards momentum the audience was invested in, and answers arrive as retroactive summary.

### 315 · Escalating Container
- **mechanism:** The world widens each season — same engine, larger arena (block → city → institution → nation).
- **engineers:** Growth without reinvention. The viewer gets novelty while the machine stays recognizably itself.
- **example:** *Succession* (stakes widen, engine identical)
- **failsWhen:** Escalation outruns the engine. The show reaches a scale its original mechanism can't generate stories at, and starts importing plot.

These TV moves are existing systems in the co-owned 1–30 corpus, applied to series. Reference the base id with a series note:

| TV move | Base system | Series note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multi-POV retelling of one event | **Rashomon** | As a single episode, not a season; usually a mid-season change-up. |
