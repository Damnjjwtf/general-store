# Tone board — the look of *General Store*

Premise-agnostic. This is the visual thesis — true whatever protagonist, city, or era we land on. The specifics (this era's exact palette, wardrobe, the actual store) get fixed once `bible/premise.md` is chosen; everything below survives that choice.

Companion to `CLAUDE.md` §3 (voice & tone). Same rule, visual form: **warm, not sentimental. The image does the work an on-the-nose line would.**

---

## The thesis, in one line
**The beauty of the well-made object.** Care shown through craft and light, never stated. If a frame looks like a housewares ad, it's wrong.

## Palette
Warm and earthy, restrained — a *shop*, not a candy store.
- **Anchors:** copper / brass, cream / bone, worn wood browns.
- **Accents:** deep forest green, oxblood, mustard, ink black.
- **Discipline:** low saturation, warm bias. One or two accents per frame, not a rainbow.
- **Avoid:** cool corporate blue, TV-bright over-saturation, neon, gloss.

## Light
- Warm and **directional** — the golden hour of a shop; north light falling on a countertop.
- **Practical sources** in frame (lamps, a window, a bulb over the workbench). Honest shadows; nothing flat or beauty-lit.
- Objects get **portrait light** — a thing lit like it matters, because in this show it does.

## Texture & material
- **Film, not digital-clean.** Grain present. A little softness at the edges.
- Materials read tactile: wood grain, enamel, tin-lined copper, checkerboard tile, hand-lettered signage, paper (a catalog, a price tag).
- **Patina over polish.** Things have been used and cared for.

## Framing — the object as hero
This is the show's signature move, made visual.
- A recurring **still-life instinct:** the single object given space, centered or rule-of-thirds, shallow-ish focus — a portrait of a whisk, a pan, a knife.
- The **teacher and the object** in the same frame — hands on the thing, the lesson happening.
- The **threshold:** crossing into the store as an event. The shop as a room you enter, not a backdrop.

## The store as a character
- Curated, hand-built, a little imperfect. Shelving that someone made. Goods arranged with intent.
- The counter as a stage where objects get explained.
- Warmth you can feel through glass from the street.

## Lens / camera language
- Warm primes; frames that **hold still** and let you look. Composition over movement.
- Movement is motivated and slow — this isn't kinetic (see the Bear caution in `CLAUDE.md`).

## Visual comps (inspiration, not to copy)
Pull the *color and light*, not the plot, from: **Julia**, **Lessons in Chemistry**, **The Durrells in Corfu**, **Halt and Catch Fire** (its warm period interiors), classic **still-life photography** of tools and food. These are ore — see `research/`. Don't replicate a frame; steal the feeling.

## The anti-look (what kills the tone)
- Housewares-commercial gloss; product-shot perfection with no soul.
- Cool corporate palettes; "premium tech" minimalism.
- Founder-hagiography framing (heroic low-angle of The Great Man).
- Anachronistic irony in the imagery; winking at the audience.
